Location & access
Costa Mujeres: What That Actually Means
North of Cancún's hotel zone — quieter, calmer, still connected

Costa Mujeres is the stretch of coastline just north of Cancún proper, facing Isla Mujeres across the Bay of Mujeres. The bay acts as a natural windbreak, which keeps the water calmer here than on the exposed Caribbean side further south. You get Caribbean-blue color without the chop.

The Catalonia is on Boulevard Vialidad Paseo Mujeres — about 30 minutes from Cancún International Airport depending on traffic. The hotel zone's restaurants and nightlife are a short taxi or Uber ride away, but you won't need them. The resort has enough on property to fill a week without feeling like you've seen everything by day two.

📍

On the ground

Cancún International Airport (CUN) is your arrival point. Immigration and customs at CUN can be slow — plan 45–60 minutes from wheels-down to your transportation. Private transfers beat shuttle buses every time here; you're not stopping at seven resorts before yours. The ride north to Costa Mujeres is smooth and direct.

💱

Currency note

Mexican law prohibits cash payment in USD at hotels. Bring a credit card (Visa, Mastercard, or Amex accepted) or exchange to pesos for tips and off-property purchases. The resort charges an Environmental Sanitation Tax of approximately $1.85 USD per person per night, payable locally. Budget for this separately.

The Restaurant Lineup
11 dining options from buffet to beachfront à la carte — here's how to navigate them
🍽️

How reservations work

À la carte restaurant reservations open on arrival via the Catalonia app. Book your preferred restaurants the moment you check in — Steakville and Ayami fill fastest. The number of à la carte dinners included in your stay varies by room category and length of stay. Privileged guests access BLoved anytime without reservations.

BLoved

À la carte · Privileged Exclusive · Beachfront

Breakfast, lunch, and dinner on the sand. The menu moves between Mediterranean and Mexican — paella alongside ceviche, ravioli next to tacos. The Catalan cream dessert shows up on enough reviews to be worth ordering. The best dining experience on property, full stop.

⭑ Insider: tuna toasts at lunch are consistently praised. Save room.
Dress code: informal for breakfast/lunch, casual for dinner

Gran Caribe

Buffet · All-Inclusive · Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

The resort's main buffet surrounds you with outdoor gardens and lake views — a better setting than a typical hotel buffet. Show cooking stations prepare dishes to order. Solid variety; reviewers call it "above average for the format." Traditional bread with tomato is a constant at breakfast.

Dress code: informal breakfast/lunch, casual dinner

Steakville

À la carte · Dinner Only

Grilled meats over charcoal — pichanha, entraña, New York strip — plus fish and vegetables for non-carnivores. American sides: lobster bisque, mashed potatoes, carrot cake. Multiple reviewers single this out as the best evening experience outside of BLoved. Book it first.

⭑ Insider: Book Steakville the day you arrive. It fills every night.
Closed shoes required. No shorts or sleeveless shirts for men.

Ayami

À la carte · Teppanyaki · Dinner Only

Live teppanyaki — the chef cooks at your table on steel griddle plates. Rice, meat, seafood, and vegetables prepared to order while you watch. High on entertainment value. Mixed reviews on the food itself (some guests find it "just ok"), but the show is genuinely engaging and the portions are generous.

Closed shoes required. No shorts or sleeveless shirts for men.

BO Barcelona Origens

À la carte · Seafood · Dinner Only

The property's seafood-focused à la carte — a modern approach to fish and shellfish with a slight Mediterranean influence. If you want something lighter than Steakville and more refined than the buffet, this is the right call. Book early in your stay.

Closed shoes required. No shorts or sleeveless shirts for men.

Adelita

À la carte · Mexican · Lunch & Dinner

The resort's Mexican restaurant covers the classics: fajitas, shrimp tacos, molcajete, tarascan soup. Dessert is a sweet corn tamale with vanilla and cinnamon. Casual dress for both meals — easier to get into than the evening specialty restaurants.

Dress code: casual for lunch and dinner

Siwichi

À la carte · Peruvian Fusion · Lunch

One of the more unusual additions to a Mexico resort menu — ceviche, ají de gallina, arroz con mariscos served at outdoor tables. Suspiro Lima for dessert. A good midday break from the buffet; less formal than the evening options.

Dress code: casual

Tao-Cat

À la carte · Pan-Asian · Dinner Only

Shrimp dumplings, spring rolls, tuna sashimi, and sushi. The menu pulls from across East and Southeast Asia rather than focusing on a single country. Closed shoes required at dinner.

Closed shoes required. No shorts or sleeveless shirts for men.

La Toscana

À la carte · Italian · Lunch

Midday pasta and wood-fired pizzas. Casual, fast, and good for families. No reservation drama — walk in for lunch and you're generally fine.

Traditional Italian with lasagna, pasta, salads, and pizza. Available with prior reservation for stays of 3+ nights. Minimum stay requirement exists — check on arrival when you have your full reservation count.

How to Actually Use the Resort
Pools, spa, entertainment — what's worth your time and what to know before you get there

Pools

  • Two main pools: one activity-focused with daily programming, one relaxation-focused with Margarita's Bar swim-up service
  • A third children's pool is separate — families can keep kids in their own zone
  • Privileged guests have exclusive pool access separate from general areas
  • Lounge chairs are saved early in the morning — a recurring complaint. Get to the pool by 8 a.m. if you want a prime spot
  • Hotel policy officially discourages chair saving, but enforcement is inconsistent. Go early or accept what's left

Beach

  • Costa Mujeres beach is protected and calmer than Caribbean-facing beaches further south — good for swimming
  • White sand, clear water, coconut palms. The beach lives up to what you see in photos
  • Privileged guests have a dedicated beach section with the Pure Bar — worth the upgrade if beach time is a priority
  • Sargassum (seaweed) can appear on any Mexican Caribbean beach seasonally (typically summer). The resort works to clear it but can't eliminate it entirely
  • Water shoes optional but useful if you plan to wade into rocky sections

Alegría Spa

  • Outdoor heated infinity pool overlooking the Caribbean — one of the more striking features on property
  • Full hydrotherapy circuit: sauna, steam bath, jacuzzi, plunge pool, hydro-massage shower
  • Beauty salon with hairdresser, manicure, and pedicure services
  • Gym with cardio, weights, and studios for spinning, yoga, and Pilates
  • No guests under 18 permitted in spa areas
  • Privileged guests: 10% off all treatments, one free humid-area access per stay (book ahead)
  • Book spa treatments on arrival — popular time slots fill within the first day

Entertainment & Activities

  • Daily pool activities run from approximately 9 a.m. through the afternoon — aerobics, volleyball, tournaments, dance classes
  • Kid's Club and Teen's Club run workshops and activities separately — families get genuinely parallel programming
  • Nightly theater performances and live entertainment rotate through the week
  • Cat's Night Club for late-night dancing (noise can carry to some room locations — ask for a room away from the disco if you're a light sleeper)
  • Scuba discovery lesson available for guests 14+ at no additional charge
  • Kayaking, snorkeling, and other water sports available from the beach
  • Excursion desk on property books off-resort experiences — compare prices with your advisor first
🐾

Wildlife on property

Agoutis — the small, brown, rabbit-like mammals native to the Yucatán — roam freely through the resort grounds. Guests consistently find this charming. They're harmless, though you'll want to keep food secured. Don't feed them.

📶

Connectivity

Wi-Fi is available throughout the resort. Speed and reliability vary by area — outdoor locations near pools and the beach tend to have weaker signal than rooms and common areas. Download anything you need before you settle in for the day poolside.

Excursions Worth Building Your Week Around
The Costa Mujeres location gives you easy access to some of the region's best day trips

Isla Mujeres

⭑ Top Priority

The island directly across the bay from your resort — visible from the beach. Just 4.3 miles long and less than a kilometer wide. Catamaran tours depart from Puerto Juárez (the closest ferry terminal to your resort). You can also take the ferry independently for about 20–30 minutes.

On the island: golf cart rental to tour the south end, Playa Norte (flat, calm, turquoise — consistently rated one of the Caribbean's best beaches), Garrafón Reef Park for snorkeling and kayaking, the Underwater Museum of Art (MUSA) with 500+ submerged sculptures, downtown for local food and shopping.

Ferry: ~20–30 min Full day recommended Bring pesos for tips

Isla Contoy

National Park · Limited Access

A protected national park north of Isla Mujeres — officially a bird sanctuary with some of the densest frigate bird, pelican, and cormorant populations in Mexico. Daily visitor numbers are capped to preserve the ecosystem. Snorkeling in the surrounding waters is excellent. Often combined with an Isla Mujeres day in tour packages.

Full day tour Book in advance Limited permits daily

Whale Shark Swimming

Seasonal: June–September

One of the few places on earth where this is possible at scale — whale sharks aggregate in the waters north of Isla Mujeres each summer, feeding on fish eggs near the surface. Tours depart from Isla Mujeres and Cancún. You enter the water with a guide and swim alongside fish that can reach 30+ feet. Snorkel only (no scuba). Bucket-list tier if your dates align.

Chichén Itzá

UNESCO World Heritage · Day Trip

One of the New Seven Wonders of the World — the Temple of Kukulcán and its surrounding archaeological complex. A 2.5–3 hour drive from Cancún. Tour packages typically include a Yucatecan buffet lunch and a cenote stop for swimming. Go early; the site gets crowded and hot by midday. A guided tour is worth the cost here — context transforms what you're seeing.

Full day Leave by 7 a.m. Bring sunscreen + water

Cenote Swimming

Natural Wonder

The Yucatán Peninsula sits on a vast underground river system, and cenotes — freshwater sinkholes — are the surface access points. The water is clear and cool, the geology is unlike anything in the Caribbean world above ground. Many are open for swimming, snorkeling, and even light cave diving. Often combined with Chichén Itzá or offered as standalone half-day tours.

Half or full day Bring biodegradable sunscreen Required at many sites

Ek Balam

Archaeological Site · Less Crowded

An alternative to Chichén Itzá that gets a fraction of the visitors — and you can still climb the main pyramid. The Sak Xok Nahh (White House of Reading) and the acropolis are remarkably well preserved. Typically combined with a cenote and local village visit. The best Mayan site choice if you've already seen Chichén Itzá.

Full day Often combined with cenote Fewer crowds than Chichén Itzá

Isla Holbox

Remote Island · Yum Balam Reserve

North of Cancún in the Yum Balam Ecological Reserve — a far quieter island than Isla Mujeres. No cars, sand streets, bioluminescent plankton at night, and a genuinely unhurried atmosphere. Whale sharks also pass through during summer. A longer day trip but a different experience entirely. Arrange transport and consider a guided tour for logistics.

Long day trip Car ferry from Chiquilá Bring cash (limited ATMs)

Mayan Museum of Cancún

Half Day · Cultural

The most important Mayan artifact collection in Quintana Roo — pieces from across the peninsula plus objects from Palenque and Chichén Itzá. The building integrates with the San Miguelito archaeological site and the Nichupté Lagoon views. Good for a morning or afternoon when you want culture without a full-day commitment.

2–3 hours Cancún Hotel Zone AC — good for hot days

What to Know Before You Go
The details that don't make it into the brochure

Booking & Check-In

  • Check-in is at 3:00 p.m. — early check-in is a Privileged benefit, subject to availability. Call ahead or note your arrival time when booking to request consideration
  • Checkout is at 12:00 p.m. — late checkout to 3:00 p.m. is a Privileged benefit (availability basis)
  • The credit card used to book must match the name of the guest — if it doesn't, contact the hotel before arrival or expect delays at check-in
  • Book restaurant reservations the moment you arrive — open the app at the front desk. Don't wait until the next morning
  • Dress codes at à la carte dinner restaurants are enforced — closed shoes and no sleeveless shirts for men. Pack accordingly; flip-flops and tank tops won't get you through the door

Packing List Additions

  • Closed-toe shoes (required for specialty dinner restaurants)
  • Reef-safe or biodegradable sunscreen — required at many cenotes and marine areas, and the right call at the resort too
  • Cash in pesos for off-property tips, local restaurants, and market purchases; pesos get better rates than dollars at local spots
  • A light layer for evenings — air conditioning at the resort and restaurants can be aggressive
  • Refillable water bottle — the resort provides water but a bottle makes pool days easier
  • Waterproof phone case if you plan to snorkel or kayak

Room Booking Strategy

  • Request a room away from the nightclub if you go to sleep before midnight — the disco runs late and sound carries in some building positions
  • High floors generally get better views and more breeze — worth requesting if available
  • The Romance Deluxe & Swim-Up room requires a 4-night minimum stay to activate the romantic dinner and couples massage inclusion — confirm your stay length qualifies before booking
  • Swim-up rooms are ground level — great for pool access, less ideal for privacy or view. Match the room to what you actually want from a stay

Making the Most of the Week

  • Book Steakville and Ayami in the first hour of arrival — they fill fastest every day of the week
  • Plan 2 resort days for every 1 full excursion day — the property has enough to keep you genuinely occupied without feeling like you're hiding from the sun
  • The catamaran tours to Isla Mujeres typically depart from Puerto Juárez, which is a short transfer from your resort. Depart by 9:00 a.m. to maximize island time
  • For Chichén Itzá: go early, go on a weekday, and bring your own snacks and water for the drive
  • Spa bookings should go in on day one — the infinity pool overlooking the sea is worth making the effort for

The quick-reference information you'll want during trip planning

  • Phone: +52 (998) 881 23 00
  • Check-in: 3:00 p.m. | Checkout: 12:00 p.m.

  • Smoking prohibited throughout the property except designated zones
  • Voltage: 110V — standard US plugs work fine

Payments & Fees

  • Accepted cards: Visa, Mastercard, Amex
  • Cash accepted only in Mexican pesos — USD cash not accepted by law
  • Environmental Sanitation Tax: ~$1.85 USD per person per night, paid locally at the hotel
  • Groups of 5+ rooms or stays of 9+ nights require advance prepayment — the hotel will contact you to arrange this
  • Credit card must be in the guest's name or contact the hotel in advance

Getting There & Around

  • Nearest airport: Cancún International (CUN) — approximately 30 minutes by car
  • Private transfers strongly recommended over shared shuttles
  • Uber and local taxis available for off-property exploration
  • Ferry to Isla Mujeres: Gran Puerto terminal in Puerto Juárez is the closest departure point — short transfer from the resort
  • A rental car opens up more flexibility for self-guided Yucatán exploration, including cenotes and smaller archaeological sites

Weather & When to Go

  • Peak season: December–April. Dry, warm, and busy. Book further ahead for best room selection
  • Shoulder season: May–June, November. Fewer crowds, good rates, still excellent weather
  • Summer (July–October): Hot and humid. Hurricane season technically runs June–November, though direct hits are rare. Whale shark season (June–September) is a significant draw if that's on your list
  • Sargassum (seaweed) is most prevalent July–October — common to the entire Mexican Caribbean, not specific to this resort
  • Water temperature stays warm year-round — 78–82°F (26–28°C)
