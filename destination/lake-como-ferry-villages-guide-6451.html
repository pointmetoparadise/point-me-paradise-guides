Lake Como: Bellagio, Menaggio & the Western Shore

A ferry pass, three villages that actually suit each other, and a pace built for lingering over aperitivo instead of chasing timetables.

Getting Your Bearings

Lake Como is shaped like an upside-down Y, and the good news is your villages sit right where the branches meet.

Bellagio sits on the point where the lake splits into its two southern arms — the most photographed spot on the water, and the one most people picture when they hear "Lake Como." Menaggio sits directly across on the western shore, a real lakeside town rather than a preserved-for-tourists center. Tremezzo and its neighbor Cadenabbia sit just south of Menaggio, known less for wandering old lanes and more for grand villas and views back across the water toward Bellagio.

Why the ferry matters here

The official ferry network connects Bellagio, Menaggio and Cadenabbia particularly well, and the boat itself becomes part of the sightseeing rather than just the way you get around. Take at least one of the slower standard ferries instead of relying only on the fast hydrofoils — it's transportation and a lake tour rolled into one.

Varenna, directly across the lake from Bellagio, and Lenno, just south of Tremezzo, round out the cluster nicely if you want to add a stop or two — both pair naturally with a ferry day built around Bellagio and the western shore, and both are covered in the sections below.

The Villages

Three villages, three different moods — and a case for giving each one its own unhurried block of time.

Bellagio

Bellagio occupies the point where the lake divides, and its historic center makes the most of that position — a maze of cobbled stairways, narrow lanes, boutiques and cafés that keep opening onto another lake view. The atmosphere is polished and unmistakably upscale, which is exactly the draw, but it's also the village most likely to feel overrun by day-trippers between late morning and mid-afternoon.

Arrive before about 9:30am or come back late in the afternoon — recent local and traveler reports consistently point to midday as the pressure point for crowds and ferry queues, especially in summer.

Menaggio

Menaggio feels like an actual lakeside town rather than a carefully preserved stage set — Piazza Garibaldi, a proper promenade, cafés and shops that see as many locals as visitors. It's a genuinely pleasant place to slow down for an afternoon, and its strong ferry and bus connections make it an easy base if you want fewer packed-bag mornings.

Open Menaggio in Google Maps

Give Menaggio more time than a typical first pass allows for — the waterfront alone rewards lingering, and it's one of the easiest ferry stops on the whole lake.

Tremezzo & Cadenabbia

These two neighboring communities on the western shore are less about wandering a medieval center and more about elegant villas, gardens and long views back across the water toward Bellagio. Villa Carlotta is the headline stop, and the Lake Como Greenway runs directly through the area if you want to keep walking afterward.

Two more worth the ferry ride

Varenna, directly across from Bellagio, is smaller, steeper and arguably more atmospheric — pastel houses tumbling toward the water, plus a direct train line to Milan if anyone's arriving that way. Lenno, just past Tremezzo, is quieter still, with a sheltered waterfront and the boat launch for Villa del Balbianello. Neither requires a special detour — both slot naturally into a day built around Bellagio and the western shore.

Top Sights

The villas and gardens that make a case for staying longer than a single day.

Villa del Balbianello

If you only visit one grand villa, this is the strongest case for it. The villa sits on a wooded peninsula jutting into the lake near Lenno, its gardens, terraces and loggia framing views across the water and toward the mountains. Its cinematic setting has made it a filming location more than once, Star Wars included.

Open Villa del Balbianello in Google Maps

Villa Carlotta

Villa Carlotta pairs an art museum with a major botanical garden — the collection includes works associated with Canova, Hayez and Thorvaldsen, while the gardens are best known for spring azaleas and rhododendrons. In 2026, the main season runs March 20 through October 18, generally 10am to 7pm, with shorter hours in autumn.

Open Villa Carlotta in Google Maps

Hours shift seasonally — confirm the current schedule before building it into a fixed itinerary.

Villa Melzi Gardens

In Bellagio, the gardens of Villa Melzi are one of the best escapes from the crowded lanes near the ferry dock — a lakeside setting with sculptures, exotic plantings and long waterfront views, better suited to travelers who'd rather wander a garden than tour a palace interior.

Punta Spartivento

Walk north through Bellagio to the tip of the peninsula and you can look straight up the northern branch of the lake toward the Alps — a good sense of exactly why Bellagio sits where it does, at the center of that inverted Y.

If you add Varenna to the day

Villa Monastero has nearly two kilometers of botanical gardens running along the water, developed from a medieval convent. Castello di Vezio sits high above town — the official route from Varenna takes roughly 30 minutes and climbs about 160 meters, so proper shoes matter more than they might seem to. The reward is one of the best elevated views on the central lake.

Food & Drink

Lake Como cooking blends freshwater fish with hearty mountain food, and it deserves more attention than it usually gets.

Look for risotto con filetti di pesce persico — risotto with perch — along with lavarello (whitefish), trout with butter and sage, missoltini (dried lake fish), polenta, mushrooms, game and local cheeses. The region also produces olive oil under the Laghi Lombardi designation, worth picking up if you're shopping in Menaggio.

Bellagio has the largest concentration of restaurants geared toward visitors — current traveler reviews keep pointing to Enoteca CavaTuracciolo and Ittiturismo Ristorante Mella, though it's always worth confirming they're open and taking reservations before you build an evening around one. In Varenna, the small size of the village means the better tables fill fast; Al Prato and Il Cavatappi are the two that keep coming up in current feedback.

Aperitivo in Menaggio

Menaggio's lake-facing Piazza Garibaldi is a natural place to end a ferry-hopping day — sit with a drink as the afternoon crowds thin out and head back toward Milan and Como. Don't treat this hour as wasted sightseeing time; it's very much part of the point.

Getting Outside

A walk that turns the western shore into scenery instead of just a ferry stop between villas.

The Lake Como Greenway is one of the better activities if you want to see the western shore beyond the ferry docks. The official route runs roughly 11.2 km through Colonno, Sala Comacina, Ossuccio, Lenno, Mezzegra, Tremezzo and Griante, taking about three and a half hours walked straight through — though it breaks easily into shorter sections. Walking just the Lenno-to-Tremezzo/Cadenabbia stretch and pairing it with a ferry back is a particularly good way to fit it in without committing the whole day.

Shopping

Bellagio for boutiques, Menaggio for something closer to everyday life.

Bellagio's stairways and lanes are the main event for shopping — silk, leather goods, jewelry, ceramics and gourmet products fill the storefronts, and Como silk makes a genuinely fitting souvenir. Menaggio's shopping runs a little more everyday: leather goods, silk, clothing, local cheeses, honey, wine and olive oil.

Market days worth building around

Timing can decide which village is worth adding to a given day. Local tourism information lists a Tuesday-morning market in Lenno and a Menaggio market on the second and fourth Friday mornings of each month — worth checking against your dates if a market is part of the appeal.

Worth the Extra Time

A short list of stops that trade the villa-and-shopping circuit for something different.

Bellano's Orrido

Bellano makes a good stand-in for another crowded afternoon in Bellagio or Varenna. Its centerpiece is the Orrido di Bellano, a gorge cut by the Pioverna River and reached via walkways suspended above the rushing water — a real change of pace from villas and boutiques.

Open Orrido di Bellano in Google Maps

Isola Comacina

Lake Como's one inhabited-history island sits opposite Ossuccio. Archaeological remains trace its importance back through the medieval period and Roman times before that — an interesting break from the villa-and-shopping rhythm of the main villages.

A Two-Day Way to See It

Two villages a day, one ferry pass, no chasing timetables.

Day One — Bellagio & the point

Start in Bellagio early, before the day-trip crowds settle in. Wander the lanes and stairways, walk to Punta Spartivento, and take your time over lunch. If a ferry over to Varenna appeals — pastel houses, a proper waterfront, Villa Monastero's gardens — it pairs naturally with a Bellagio morning.

Day Two — The western shore

Ferry to Lenno for Villa del Balbianello, walk a stretch of the Greenway toward Tremezzo, and stop at Villa Carlotta along the way. Finish in Menaggio: a walk along the promenade, then aperitivo in Piazza Garibaldi as the day-trip traffic thins out and heads back toward Milan.

Good to Know

A few practical notes worth reading before you land.

Ferries

Always check the official Navigazione Laghi timetable for your actual travel dates — schedules shift seasonally, and weekdays run differently than Sundays and holidays. The operator recommends reaching ticket offices about 20 minutes before departure, and is upfront that summer and holiday traffic can mean real queues. Build in extra cushion around Bellagio and Varenna in July and August.

Official ferry schedules & tickets

Best time to go

April–May and September–early October give you the nicest balance of scenery, open attractions and manageable temperatures — May is especially good for the gardens. June is lovely but busier, and July–August bring the heaviest ferry pressure, heat and crowds. September is one of the strongest overall choices.

Getting around on foot

Bellagio and Varenna both involve cobbles, stairs and steep grades, and Castello di Vezio is genuinely unsuitable if stairs are a problem. Menaggio's lakefront is considerably easier going if that matters for your group. Check individual villa gardens for accessible routes if needed.

Lake Como is polished, but it's still Italian rather than a resort complex — lunch and dinner run on Italian schedules, churches expect respectful clothing, and smaller places may close between meals. A little Italian goes a long way: buongiorno, buonasera, grazie, per favore.

Worth avoiding

Skip driving between the central villages during peak season if you can — roads are narrow, parking is tight, and Bellagio's center includes a restricted-traffic zone. Don't schedule Bellagio for 11am–3pm in July or August if you have flexibility; that's when queues and crowd pressure peak. And don't try to collect too many villages in one day — three ferry stops plus two villas sounds efficient on paper and turns into a scramble in practice. Skip flimsy sandals for anything involving Varenna's uphill walks.
