Cabo: A Traveler's Guide to Los Cabos

Two towns, one arch, and a coastline where the desert meets two different oceans.

Los Cabos is the name for the whole region sitting at the very southern tip of the Baja California peninsula, and it's really two towns pretending to be one destination. Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo sit about twenty miles apart, connected by the Tourist Corridor, and they could not have more different personalities. Everything below is organized so you can figure out which version of Cabo you actually want — and which parts of the other one are still worth the drive.

Orientation & Character

Same peninsula, two very different vacations.

Local Favorite

Cabo San Lucas

Cabo San Lucas is the livelier, more extroverted half of the pair — a magnet for anyone looking to fold some adrenaline into a beach trip, whether that's a boat tour by day or bars that run until sunrise. The town is built around a marina that's always at full capacity, and it draws a college-aged crowd, young couples on quick getaways, and families who came for the water sports. All-inclusive properties here tend to run a little less polished than their San José counterparts, which is part of the trade for being steps from everything.

📍 Cabo San Lucas Marina
Local Favorite

San José del Cabo

Twenty miles northeast, San José del Cabo trades marina nightlife for cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, and a main square — Plaza Mijares — that anchors a historic center the Mexican government has officially designated a Barrio Mágico for its preserved cultural character. El Centro is a walkable grid built around the 18th-century Misión de San José del Cabo, with bougainvillea and murals lining the way. The Gallery District along Calle Obregón runs about four blocks of galleries, boutique restaurants, and independent shops, and every Thursday night from November through June the street closes to traffic for the Art Walk, when galleries stay open until 9 p.m.

📍 Plaza Mijares, San José del Cabo

Honest caveat: San José doesn't have much going on after 10 p.m., and you'll want a car — the best beaches and restaurants aren't within walking distance of El Centro.

Which one should you base yourself in?

Choose Cabo San Lucas if you want all-inclusive ease, quick access to nightlife, and a room a short walk from the marina. Choose San José if you'd rather have intimate design, artsy neighborhoods, and a shorter hop from the airport.

Top Sights & Experiences

The landmark that gives the whole cape its name, and the water around it.

Must Do

El Arco / The Arch (Land's End)

This granite formation towers three stories above the point where the Pacific Ocean meets the Sea of Cortés, and it's the single most recognizable symbol of Los Cabos. You can only reach it by boat — from Medano Beach or the Cabo San Lucas Marina — which is part of what keeps it feeling like an event rather than a drive-up photo stop.

📍 El Arco / Land's End
Must Do

Medano Beach (Playa El Médano)

The water around Cabo San Lucas can be wild in a lot of places, but Medano is the exception — calm, safe swimming right in the middle of everything. It's a long stretch of golden sand with towels, umbrellas, volleyball games, and beach bars, backed by resorts and high-rises, and it's the launch point for most boat tours out to the Arch.

📍 Medano Beach

Lover's Beach & Divorce Beach

These two sit on either side of the Arch and could not feel more different. Lover's Beach has calm, turquoise water and soft sand — good for swimming and snorkeling. Divorce Beach, just around the point, has rocky cliffs and intense waves that make it too dangerous to swim.

📍 Lover's Beach
📍 Divorce Beach

This is not a swimming beach — stay out of the water at Divorce Beach.

Local Favorite

Pelican Rock

Off the coast at Land's End, Pelican Rock is a protected spot with dramatic rock formations and a small, gravelly beach that draws snorkelers, swimmers, scuba divers, and cliff jumpers. It's known for how much wildlife shows up here, both above and below the surface.

Must Do — Seasonal

Whale Watching

Each winter, humpback and gray whales migrate down from the Arctic into the waters around Los Cabos, and December through April is the season for it. January through March is the peak window, with February giving you the best odds of a close encounter. Tours run out of the Cabo San Lucas Marina.

📍 Cabo San Lucas Marina (tour departure point)
Hidden Gem

Cerro del Vigía (Mt. Solmar hike)

A short but genuinely challenging hike up to one of the best views in Cabo San Lucas — a 360-degree look over the Arch and the exact point where the Pacific gives way to the Sea of Cortés.

Food & Drink Highlights

The Marlin Capital of the World, and where the tacos are actually good.

Cabo's two signature dishes are the Baja fish taco and smoked marlin. A proper Baja-style fish taco is crispy, beer-battered white fish in a warm corn tortilla — never flour, if it's authentic — topped with shredded cabbage, pico de gallo, and a chipotle sauce. And as the self-declared Marlin Capital of the World, Cabo does smoked marlin well: typically shredded and served on tostadas, or as a savory pâté with crackers and spicy salsa.

Local Favorite

Taquería El Estadio

A locals-first taquería built around marlín — smoked, seasoned, and served in corn tortillas with a build-it-yourself condiment bar. At 35–45 pesos a taco, this is Cabo's signature dish in its actual local form, not the version built for the marina crowd. Go at lunch — you'll be eating alongside construction workers, fishermen, and hotel staff on break. There's no English menu, so plan to point and order.

📍 Taquería El Estadio, Cabo San Lucas
Local Favorite

Los Claros

A few blocks off the main tourist strip downtown, Los Claros does Sinaloa-style seafood tacos on flour tortillas, with coleslaw, pickled onions, pico de gallo, and a salsa bar to build your own. Order the smoked marlín tacos and the lightly-breaded fish tacos.

📍 Los Claros, Cabo San Lucas

Tacos Gardenias

A small, mom-and-pop spot with gamefish mounted on the walls that's been a neighborhood favorite for 30 years. Go for the fish and shrimp tacos, ceviche, seafood soup, or the shrimp molcajete — a red stew of shrimp, peppers, cheese, and onion served in a stone mortar.

Must Do

Metate

A Michelin Bib Gourmand recipient on the tourist corridor in El Tezal, doing traditional wood-fired Mexican cooking and handmade tortillas in a rustic, tree-shaded, string-lit setting. The Baja Taco — battered fish tempura, purple cabbage slaw, avocado, cilantro — is the one to order.

📍 Metate, Cabo San Lucas

Late-night, cash-only

Tacos El Paisa doesn't get busy until after midnight, peaking around 2–3 a.m. when the bars close. Order the tacos de suadero (slow-cooked brisket) and tacos de cabeza. Cash only, standing room only.

Fine dining, San José del Cabo side

Sushi Luna's omakase draws from the local tuna fishing fleet and has earned its reputation for it. Cocina de Autor, at Grand Velas Los Cabos, holds the only Michelin star in Los Cabos.

If you need something meat-free

Tropical Kitchen is an organic spot serving farm-to-table Buddha bowls and vegan açaí with fresh, locally sourced ingredients.

The better food in Cabo San Lucas is usually found one block off the water — the marina-front restaurants are built for the tourist traffic passing right in front of them, and prices and quality reflect that.

Outdoor & Nature

Desert mountains a world away from the marina, twenty minutes inland.

Hidden Gem

Sierra de la Laguna

A UNESCO-designated biosphere reserve inland from the coast, with cool pine forests and cascading waterfalls that feel like a different country from the coastal desert. Canyon de la Zorra has become a year-round hiking destination, where freshwater flows over huge granite boulders and forms pools you can actually swim in — a genuine surprise to find in the middle of the desert.

📍 Sierra de la Laguna
Must Do — Seasonal

Swimming with whale sharks

Tours run seasonally, December through April, alongside the whale watching season — peak odds are January through March.

Must Do — Day Trip

Todos Santos

A Pueblo Mágico worth the drive, with cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, art galleries and craft shops, regional cuisine, and white sand beaches. Activities here run from surfing and hiking to whale watching and horseback riding.

📍 Todos Santos, Baja California Sur

Pacific side vs. Sea of Cortés side

Worth factoring into where you stay: the Pacific side of the peninsula runs noticeably cooler and windier than the Sea of Cortés side.

Shopping & Markets

Silver, Talavera, and a Thursday night that's worth building a dinner around.

Must Do

Marina Mercado

Along the waterfront in downtown Cabo San Lucas, this market has been a fixture of the community for over two decades — it started as a gathering spot for classic car enthusiasts and has since become a hub for local artisans selling ironwood carvings, ceramics, silver jewelry, and Talavera pottery.

📍 Marina Mercado, Cabo San Lucas

Cabo is a good place for real silver — look for pieces stamped "925," which indicates sterling silver.

Must Do — Seasonal, Thursday Evenings

The Art District / Gallery District, San José del Cabo

Along Calle Obregón, this is the top cultural shopping experience in the region. The Thursday Art Walk, running November through June, closes the street to traffic and fills it with local markets, textiles, silver jewelry, and handmade crafts against a backdrop of colonial architecture and cobblestone streets.

Local Favorite — Seasonal Saturdays

San José del Cabo Organic Farmers Market

In the Huerta María community space, running Saturdays from late October through May. Expect farm-fresh produce, handmade cheeses, natural soaps, baked goods, jewelry, crafts, and live music.

El Merkado

Along the tourist corridor between the two towns, this is less a street market and more a modern food hall — a two-floor space shared by different restaurants, bars, and specialty vendors, open daily.

Carry cash for smaller purchases and out-of-the-way shops, where card machines can be unreliable. Major credit cards work at established businesses, but many smaller vendors and artisan markets are cash-only. Pesos are preferred everywhere; some tourist-area shops take U.S. dollars, but usually at an unfavorable rate.

Hidden Gems & Local Tips

The version of Cabo that isn't built around the marina.

  • Cerro del Vigía hike — the 360-degree Arch and Land's End view, without needing a boat.
  • Canyon de la Zorra — freshwater pools in the Sierra de la Laguna that most visitors never see.
  • Taquería El Estadio and similar no-English-menu, cash-only marlín spots — where locals actually eat, versus the marina's tourist-facing versions.
  • The Thursday night Art Walk in San José del Cabo (November–June) — free, low-key, and a genuine mix of locals, expats, and travelers.

Practical Information

What to know before you land.

Getting here and around

Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo are linked by the Tourist Corridor highway. You'll generally want a car or private driver to move between the two towns and to reach the better beaches and restaurants outside San José's walkable centro.

Best time to visit

The dry season, November through May, brings the most comfortable temperatures — low-to-mid 80s°F — very little rain, and calm ocean conditions. It's also peak season, meaning the biggest crowds and highest prices, and whale migration peaks in February. May, June, and early October tend to offer the best balance of good weather and smaller crowds.

Hurricane season

Los Cabos sits in the eastern Pacific hurricane basin. The official season runs May 15 through November 30, with activity concentrated from late June into early October and the highest risk in August and September. Most visitors traveling in this window experience no disruption at all, but travel insurance with trip-interruption coverage is worth having if you're traveling August through October. Direct hits are rare but not unheard of — Hurricane Odile made landfall here in 2014 and caused significant damage.

Currency

The official currency is the Mexican peso, though U.S. dollars are widely accepted. You'll generally come out ahead converting cash to pesos — the exchange rate on dollars tends to be unfavorable, and change is given in pesos regardless of what you pay with.

Safety

As of 2026, Baja California Sur — which includes both Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo — carries a U.S. State Department Level 2 "Exercise Increased Caution" advisory, the same rating currently given to France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Belgium, and Japan. That means: stay aware of your surroundings, not stay away. In February 2026, the U.S. Consulate issued a security alert noting incidents between criminal elements and law enforcement near the Los Cabos airport, outside the traditional tourist areas, and advised continued vigilance, particularly around the airport. U.S. government employees traveling here are restricted from moving between cities after dark. One practical note: Uber can drop you at the airport but isn't permitted to pick up passengers there — use a clearly identifiable, numbered taxi when you're leaving.

The real risk here is the ocean, not crime

Water-related incidents account for the majority of serious tourist accidents and fatalities in Los Cabos. Many Pacific-facing beaches have powerful rip currents, undertow, and steep drop-offs. The reliably safe beaches for swimming are Medano Beach, Chileno Bay, Santa María Bay, and Palmilla Beach.

What to Avoid

A short list, worth reading before you land.

Avoid

Swimming at Divorce Beach, Pacific-facing beaches, or any beach without posted flags — riptides, undertow, and rogue waves are common here. Pay attention to the color-coded flag system and stick to designated swimming beaches.

Avoid

Marina-front restaurants advertising "smoked marlin" and fish tacos to cruise and resort traffic. Better versions exist one block off the water at local prices.

Avoid

Flagging down unmarked street taxis, especially late at night. Stick to reputable transportation — official numbered taxis, pre-booked transfers, or Uber where it's permitted to operate.

Avoid

Leaving drinks unattended in nightlife areas. Drink spiking has been reported at some Cabo San Lucas clubs.
