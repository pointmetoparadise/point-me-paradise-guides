Cozumel: Your Royal Caribbean Port Day

Clear water on one side of the island, wild surf on the other, and a ship to be back on by all-aboard.

Cozumel is Mexico's busiest cruise port, and on a full day you'll share it with several other ships. Most of the good stuff sits on the calm west coast, a short taxi ride south of where Royal Caribbean docks. Plan around three things and the day takes care of itself: which pier you're at, what time the ship thinks it is, and how far you're willing to go.

Where You'll Dock

Royal Caribbean ships tie up south of town, not downtown.

You'll walk straight off the gangway onto a pier. There's no tender boat. Royal Caribbean usually docks at the International Pier, about 3 miles (4 km) south of downtown San Miguel. You come off into a large terminal plaza with duty-free shops, souvenir stalls, bars and a taxi stand with the fares posted on a board.

Open International Pier in Google Maps

Smaller ships, or ships arriving on days when International is full, sometimes go to Puerta Maya instead. It's right next door, about 5–10 minutes on foot. Taxi fares from either pier are essentially the same.

Good to know: If your tour's meeting point says "Puerta Maya" and you docked at International (or the other way around), it's a short walk, not a problem.

Getting around

Taxis are the main way to get around, and there's no Uber on Cozumel. Fares from the piers are set by destination and charged per cab for up to four people, not per person. Confirm the fare before you get in; paying in pesos is often a little cheaper. Typical one-way fares from International: about $8 to downtown, $12 to Chankanaab, $15–20 to the southern beach clubs and about $35 to Punta Sur. Fares change, so go by the posted board.

Oasis-class and Icon-class ships carry several thousand guests, and most of them head down the gangway within the same hour or two. With seven or eight ships in port on a busy day, the taxi line can get long. One local tip: walk out toward the main road to find a cab faster.

Ship Time Is the Only Time

The clock on your phone may be an hour off from the ship's.

Cozumel stays on the same time all year and doesn't use daylight saving. Royal Caribbean generally keeps ship time, usually the time zone of your home port, though the captain can change it. Your phone will grab a Mexican tower the moment you're near land, and that's how people end up an hour late.

Before you go ashore

Check the all-aboard time in the Royal app the night before. It's typically 30 minutes before departure, but the app is what counts. Set your watch or phone to ship time and turn off automatic time-zone updates. When you book an independent tour, give the operator your ship name and date rather than a clock time.

Port calls typically run 6–10 hours. Ships usually arrive between 7 and 8 AM and leave between 4 and 6 PM. Expect to actually step ashore 30–60 minutes after the listed arrival time.

In the Water

Cozumel's reef is the reason most people come.

El Cielo and the reef by boat

El Cielo is a shallow sandbar about 3–6 feet deep, with seagrass and starfish scattered across it. You can only get there by boat, about 20 minutes from the pier. Most trips pair it with snorkel stops at Palancar, Colombia or Paraíso reefs. Because it sits inside the reef, the water at El Cielo is typically calm even on windier days. Shared boat trips typically run about $50–55 per person; a private boat for five or six people runs roughly $460–572.

Open Playa El Cielo in Google Maps

Good to know: Touching the starfish is prohibited, and only licensed operators are allowed in. Use reef-safe sunscreen only; some catamarans don't allow any sunscreen while you snorkel, so a rash guard is worth packing.

Shore snorkeling: Money Bar and Sky Reef

For the cheapest good day in the water, take a taxi to one of these two and walk in from the beach. Money Bar, at Km 6.5, has free entry with food and drinks ordered à la carte, and it sits right on Dzul-Ha reef. It tends to be quiet, with loungers and umbrellas. Sky Reef, at Km 10, is easier to get into the water from and also has good snorkeling. It offers an all-inclusive package (about $65) or à la carte.

Open Money Bar in Google Maps Open Sky Reef in Google Maps

Good to know: At Money Bar, regulars start at the far end and let the current carry them back along the reef. At Sky Reef, staff push the all-inclusive package hard; à la carte is fine if you'd rather. There's not much for kids to do there beyond snorkeling.

The marine park fee

If you're going into the water from a boat, you'll pay a mandatory federal marine park fee, about 220–225 pesos ($11–13) per person at last check. It's often paid in cash at the marina in exchange for a wristband. Bring small bills in pesos or dollars. Chankanaab's admission already covers it.

Beach Clubs

A chair, a pool and lunch, with day passes that change price often.

The west-coast beach clubs all face the calm side of the island, and their beaches have stayed largely free of sargassum through 2026. Prices change often, so treat the figures below as a starting point. Bring a towel from the ship.

Paradise Beach

A big heated pool, water toys and a family crowd. You can pay a $15 entry plus a $10 food-and-drink minimum, buy a Fun Pass for $18, or go all-inclusive for roughly $68–81. Open 8 AM to 5 PM. It's typically a $15–18 taxi from the pier.

Open Paradise Beach in Google Maps

Mr. Sancho's

Cozumel's best-known beach club, and its liveliest. The all-inclusive pass runs roughly $68–79, and there's a water park for an extra charge. It's at Km 15, a $17–20 taxi from International.

Open Mr. Sancho's in Google Maps

Good to know: One source lists it as closed on Sundays. If your port day falls on a Sunday, check before you go.

Nachi Cocom

The quiet choice. Nachi Cocom caps attendance at about 130 guests a day, which makes it a calmer day for adults and couples. All-inclusive runs roughly $65–69. It's at Km 16.5.

Open Nachi Cocom in Google Maps

Good to know: Reservations are required. On busy ship days, a cancelled spot can be hard to rebook.

Playa Mia is gone

If you've seen Playa Mia in older guides, it's no longer an option. The site is being rebuilt as Royal Beach Club Cozumel (more on that below).

Parks and Nature

Two state-run parks, and both close on Sundays.

Chankanaab Adventure Beach Park

Ten to fifteen minutes south of the pier, inside the national reef marine park. You get a lagoon, snorkeling right off the shore, an underwater statue garden, a botanical garden, a sea lion show and a tequila experience, plus showers and lockers. Admission is $31 for adults and $22 for children 4–12. Open Monday to Saturday, 8 AM to 4 PM. Extras include snorkel gear ($15), a dolphin encounter ($109), a manatee swim ($79), Snuba ($65) and Sea Trek ($84).

Open Chankanaab in Google Maps

Good to know: Get there before 9:30 AM. The excursion buses arrive mid-morning, and on multi-ship days the park is packed from about 10:30 to 2:00. Closed Sundays.

Punta Sur Eco Beach Park

A reserve covering the island's southern tip, about 17 miles (27 km) from the pier. Admission ($25 adults, $17 children 4–12) covers the Celarain Lighthouse and its navigation museum, a viewing tower over a crocodile lagoon, a small Maya shrine, a boat ride across the lagoon and beaches with chairs. Open Monday to Saturday, 9 AM to 5 PM; several sources say the beach closes at 4.

Open Punta Sur in Google Maps

Good to know: It's about a $35 taxi each way, and return taxis are scarce this far south. Arrange your ride back before you go, or come on a jeep or private tour. Arrive at the 9 AM opening if you want to see wildlife. Closed Sundays.

Sargassum

The seaweed that has hit mainland beaches mostly lands on Cozumel's east coast. The west-coast beaches, clubs and reefs, which is where you'll spend most of the day, have stayed largely clear through 2026.

Ruins and Town

Maya history inland, and the island's only town along the water.

San Gervasio

Cozumel's largest Maya site, in the jungle at the center of the island. It was once a pilgrimage center for Ixchel, goddess of fertility and medicine, and its small temples are linked by old white limestone roads. It's modest next to the big mainland sites, but it's the real thing and you won't need a ferry. Admission is about $14.50; allow 1.5–2 hours. It's typically a $20–26 taxi each way, and it's often bundled into jeep tours that continue to the east coast.

Open San Gervasio in Google Maps

Good to know: Posted hours differ between sources (8 AM–4 PM vs. 9 AM–3:45 PM), so don't plan a late arrival. There are few signs on site, so a guide helps.

Downtown San Miguel

San Miguel is Cozumel's only town, about an $8 taxi north of the pier. The waterfront avenue, Rafael E. Melgar, is lined with shops and restaurants. Plaza del Sol is the main square. Walk two to four blocks back from the water for lower prices and a more local feel.

Open Plaza del Sol in Google Maps

Museo de la Isla de Cozumel

On the waterfront, in one of the island's first hotels from the 1930s. Its eleven interactive rooms cover the reef, Maya history, pirates and Carnaval, and an hour is about right. Admission is $11. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 9 AM to 5 PM.

Open Museo de la Isla in Google Maps

Good to know: Closed Mondays, and some listings show different hours, so check before you go.

Shop downtown, not at the pier

The pier shops are convenient, but prices there run noticeably higher than in town. If you want to shop, do it downtown and save the pier for the last half hour before you reboard.

Royal Beach Club Cozumel

Royal Caribbean's own beach club, with an opening date that isn't settled yet.

Royal Beach Club Cozumel is being built on the old Playa Mia site, about 20 minutes by bus from the port. Plans call for 42 acres and about a third of a mile of beach, with:

  • two pools, the main one with a swim-up bar
  • six bars and three dining spots, including a food hall called The Mercato
  • a street market and a Maya cacao museum
  • kayaking, snorkeling, tequila tastings and cooking classes

It's for Royal Caribbean guests only and will be booked as a paid shore excursion, with food and drinks included in the day pass.

Open Royal Beach Club Cozumel in Google Maps

What isn't settled yet

Most 2026 reports give an opening date of December 31, 2026, but other reports conflict. Pricing and the pass tiers haven't been confirmed. If your sailing is on or after that date, look in the Royal app's excursion listings for your port day to see whether it's bookable and what it costs. If it doesn't appear there, it isn't available on your date.

Royal Caribbean's Perfect Day Mexico is a separate project at Costa Maya, targeted for 2027. It is not in Cozumel.

The Wild East Side

The windward coast is for the view and lunch, not for swimming.

Across the island, the coast faces the open Caribbean: rocky shoreline, big surf and very few people. A handful of beach bars line the road. Punta Morena has saltwater pools and hammocks. Coconuts is a clifftop bar known for its views and strong margaritas; there's no beach below it. Chen Río has a protected cove. Most people get here by jeep, private taxi or an east-coast bar-hop tour.

Stay out of the surf here

This is not a swimming coast. Riptides and undertow are strong, and there's usually no lifeguard. The businesses run without grid power and typically keep roughly 10 AM–6 PM hours. There's no cell service, so if a rental car breaks down out here, help can be a long wait. Leave yourself plenty of time to drive back to the pier.

Booking Your Day

The deciding question is how far from the pier you're going.

If you book an excursion through Royal Caribbean and it runs late, the ship accounts for it. If an independent tour runs late, the ship has no obligation to wait. Ship excursions usually cost more for the same activity, and part of what that extra money buys is that protection.

For close-in plans, such as a snorkel trip, a beach club or Chankanaab, a well-reviewed independent operator with pier pickup is a low-risk choice. Most reputable operators include pickup at the pier. A private driver typically runs about $200 for five hours, which only pays off if you're making four or more stops.

The mainland: book it through the ship or skip it

Tulum, Xcaret, Xel-Há and Playa del Carmen all require a 45-minute ferry each way, plus about an hour by road on the mainland side. Plan on at least six hours off the ship. The crossing is often rough, and cruise forums are full of late returns, even on ship-run trips. If you go, book through Royal Caribbean. Plenty of cruisers who've done it say they'd stay on Cozumel next time.

If you rent a jeep for the island loop, it takes about 1.5 hours to drive without stops. Local "full coverage" rental insurance has been reported to have loopholes, and cruise forums commonly warn against renting scooters.

Before You Step Off

A short list that saves you trouble later.

What to carry

  • Your SeaPass card, in a waterproof pouch
  • Photo ID
  • Small bills, pesos or dollars, for taxis, tips and the marine park fee
  • Reef-safe sunscreen and a towel from the ship

Dollars are widely accepted, but paying in pesos often gets you a slightly better price.

Drinks and souvenirs

Your drink package doesn't apply at bars or beach clubs ashore. Whether it will apply at Royal Beach Club Cozumel hasn't been announced. Bottles of alcohol you buy ashore are normally held by the ship and returned at the end of your cruise; check the current policy before you buy.

Sundays and Mondays

Chankanaab and Punta Sur close on Sundays. The Museo de la Isla closes on Mondays. Check your port day against these before you plan around any of them.

Safety

The U.S. State Department rates Quintana Roo, the state Cozumel belongs to, at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution. That's the same level as France and the United Kingdom. The usual annoyances here are petty theft, taxi overcharging and timeshare sales pitches near the piers. The State Department suggests the Quintana Roo Guest Assist app for bilingual help.

Mexico's cruise passenger fee is collected by Royal Caribbean in advance, so there's nothing to pay at the pier.
