Sint Maarten: Your Port Day in Philipsburg

Two nations on one small island, a town you can walk to, and traffic that decides the rest of your day.

Your ship ties up in Great Bay, a mile south of Philipsburg, the capital of the Dutch side. Everything worth doing here sorts into two piles: what you can reach on foot or by water taxi, and what sits on the far side of the island's traffic. Choose your pile before you step off the gangway.

Arriving: The Pier and the Way Into Town

You dock, you don't tender, and town is a short walk or a shorter boat ride away.

Ships berth at the Dr. A.C. Wathey cruise facility, two parallel piers that handle more than two million cruise visitors a year. Step off and you're in Harbour Point Village, a replica of Philipsburg's old Caribbean-style streetscape with duty-free shops, cafés, musicians, Wi-Fi, an ATM and restrooms. It is also where excursions gather and taxis line up, and on a multi-ship morning it can be hard to find your meeting point.

Tip: If you've booked a tour, give yourself extra time to find the pickup point when several ships are in.

On foot

Port walkableDIY friendly

Turn left out of the port gates and follow the harbour. A sidewalk carries you to the Bobby's Marina end of downtown, and most people reach town and Great Bay Beach in 15 to 20 minutes. There's little shade, and the sun comes straight off the water.

Tip: Bring a hat and a full water bottle. Count on closer to 30 minutes if you're heading to the far end of town by the Courthouse.

By water taxi

DIY friendly

With your back to the piers, the water taxi terminal is on the left past the line of shop stalls. The ride takes about five minutes, and a $7 all-day pass lets you hop on and off. The most recent reports list three stops: Bobby's Marina at the start of town, Captain Hodge Pier in the middle of Great Bay Beach, and Walter Plantz Wharf at the far end of the Boardwalk.

Captain Hodge Pier on Google Maps Bobby's Marina on Google Maps

Tip: A single-ride fare has been reported at $5, but the day pass is the better buy if you'll ride more than once. Confirm current fares at the booth.

Crossing to the French side

There is no border control between Dutch Sint Maarten and French Saint Martin. You simply drive across. Carry your passport anyway, as you would for any port day.

Your Port Day Clock

The island is small; its roads are not fast.

Sint Maarten covers about 37 square miles, but bottlenecks around Simpson Bay and the border can turn a 10-minute drive into a 90-minute crawl. When the Simpson Bay drawbridge opens for yachts, traffic can back up past the airport, and a 20-minute wait at the bridge is not unusual. A newer bridge gives drivers a second route, which helps, but doesn't erase the problem.

The two-hour rule

Leave any far-side beach at least two hours before all-aboard. One longtime visitor builds in two extra hours on every trip: one for a miscalculation, one for bad traffic. Check how many ships are in port the night before. On a heavy day, the west end around Maho and Mullet Bay is the slowest corner of the island, and a boat trip from a marina you can walk to sidesteps the roads entirely.

Tip: Bridge opening times change. Ask your driver about them before you commit to the west end.

Philipsburg on Foot

Four parallel streets, a beach on one side and a salt pond on the other.

Front Street and Back Street

Port walkableDIY friendlyCultural experience

Philipsburg is laid out as a tidy grid between Great Bay Beach and Great Salt Pond. Front Street is the duty-free strip: rum cakes, perfume, souvenirs. One block back, Back Street trades the glitter for small boutiques and cafés and gives you a better look at everyday island life. Most shops open whenever a ship is in port.

Front Street on Google Maps

The Courthouse

Port walkableHistoric site

The small white Courthouse dates to 1793 and began as the home of Commander John Philips, the town's founder. It's the building on the island's 100-guilder note, so take a look before you see one in your change.

Philipsburg Courthouse on Google Maps

Guavaberry Emporium

Port walkableCultural experience

Guavaberry is the island's folk liqueur, made with oak-aged rum and wild guavaberries, and this Front Street shop is its main retailer. It sits across the street from the Sint Maarten Museum, so the two make an easy pair.

Guavaberry Emporium on Google Maps

Tip: Free tastings have been offered in the past. Ask at the counter whether they're running on your day.

Sint Maarten Museum

Port walkableHistoric site

A small heritage museum run by the National Heritage Foundation, with Arawak pottery, old photographs and artifacts from the island's past. It's a quiet half hour off the busiest stretch of Front Street.

Sint Maarten Museum on Google Maps

Tip: Current opening hours weren't available when this guide was written. Check at the door before planning around it.

The Boardwalk

A broad pedestrian promenade runs about a mile and a half along Great Bay, lined with beach bars and parasols. It's the easiest place in town to sit with a cold drink and watch the ships in the harbour, yours included.

Great Bay Beach

The closest sand to your ship, and the simplest beach day on the island.

Port walkableDIY friendlyBeach

Great Bay Beach runs along the front of Philipsburg, with calm, clear water that suits families and swimmers who don't want surf. Chairs and umbrellas are rented mostly by the Boardwalk restaurants across the sand. Expect to pay about $15 to $20 for two chairs, an umbrella and a Wi-Fi code for the day, or $25 to $30 with a bucket of beer included.

Great Bay Beach on Google Maps

Tip: Take the water taxi to Captain Hodge Pier and you step off in the middle of the beach. At least one bar a couple of blocks from the pier sells a pre-bookable package for two, around $60 with chairs, umbrella, drinks, Wi-Fi and washroom access.

The honest tradeoff

There are prettier beaches on the island. None of them can be reached without a taxi and a traffic gamble. If your time in port is short, this is the one.

Fort Amsterdam and Little Bay

A ruined fort, a pelican colony and a quiet cove, ten minutes from the pier.

Fort Amsterdam

DIY friendlyHistoric siteNature

The Dutch built Fort Amsterdam in 1631 on the peninsula at the western edge of Great Bay. What remains is ruins and a row of 19th-century cannons pointed at the sea, with views back across the bay to Philipsburg and your ship. Brown pelicans nest on the slope below, in a designated Important Bird and Biodiversity Area. Entry is free.

Fort Amsterdam on Google Maps

Tip: It's about 10 minutes by taxi from the pier. The route runs through the Divi Little Bay Beach Resort: visitors typically leave the car at the gate and walk about five minutes, mostly uphill, through the grounds.

Little Bay Beach

DIY friendlyBeach

The sheltered cove below the fort is a calmer alternative to Great Bay. It's reached by footpath only, so it isn't wheelchair accessible, and there's no lifeguard.

Little Bay Beach on Google Maps

Which fort?

Opinions split. One island site argues Fort Louis in Marigot, on the French side, is the only fort worth your time. Most visitors to Fort Amsterdam come away happy, especially about the views, and it's far closer to your ship.

On the Water

Boats skip the traffic, which makes them the safest bet on a crowded day.

12 Metre Challenge

Port walkableCruise excursionCultural experience

You crew a retired America's Cup racing yacht in a real race across the bay, trimming sails, grinding winches or punching a stopwatch, or you can simply ride along. The fleet includes Stars & Stripes 87, the boat that won the Cup back for the United States in 1987. The race runs about two and a half hours and leaves from Bobby's Marina, roughly a 10-minute walk from the pier.

12 Metre Challenge on Google Maps

Tip: Minimum age is 9, it's not wheelchair, scooter or walker accessible, and you shouldn't be prone to seasickness. It's been sold at $131 through a third-party booker; check current pricing at 12metre.com or +1 721 542-0045. One reviewer found it more of a workout than expected.

Beach-hopping and catamaran trips

Cruise excursionPrivate tour optionBeach

A popular six-hour speedboat tour with Captain Bob's strings together Tintamarre for turtles, Pinel Island, Creole Rock, Mullet Bay and Maho, with an on-time return guaranteed. For something slower, a small-group 52-foot catamaran (30 guests at most) stops at Mullet Bay and Little Bay.

Why the water wins

The 12 Metre Challenge is the most time-safe marquee experience in port: you walk there, you sail, you walk back. On a day with many ships, that matters more than any beach.

Into the Hills: Rainforest Adventures

A chairlift up Sentry Hill and a fast chair-seat zip line back down.

Cruise excursionNatureCultural experienceWatch the clock

At Rockland Estate, the Sky Explorer chairlifts carry you up Sentry Hill in about 25 minutes. Then you're strapped into a harnessed chair on the Flying Dutchman, which the operator bills as the world's steepest zip line, dropping 1,050 feet in elevation over 2,800 feet. On site, the Emilio Wilson Museum tells the story of Trace Wilson, born into slavery, and her descendant Emilio Wilson. The estate is about 30 minutes from the pier.

Rainforest Adventures on Google Maps

Tip: Riders must be 52 to 80 inches tall and weigh 100 to 275 lb, and closed-toe shoes are mandatory. The operator's site lists $109 for adults and $94 for children 3–12 and seniors 65+.

The timing catch

The park opens at 11:00 am and closes at 4:00 pm, and a visit runs 1.5 to 3 hours depending on crowds, including a mandatory safety briefing. If your ship docks late or leaves early, that window gets tight fast.

The West End: Maho and Mullet Bay

Low planes, good sand and the island's worst traffic, all in one corner.

Maho Beach

Cruise excursionDIY friendlyBeachFar from port

Planes landing at Princess Juliana Airport pass low over this small beach on their final approach. It's crowded on ship days, and jet blast throws sand. The calmer way to watch is from Sunset Bar & Grill, which posts the day's arrivals on a board, or from the Driftwood Boat Bar at the other end of the beach.

Maho Beach on Google Maps Sunset Bar & Grill on Google Maps

Tip: Jet traffic is lighter than it once was, and the 747s no longer come in. A pre-booked shuttle with a return guarantee typically leaves the port at 10:00 am and collects you from the beach at 2:45 pm.

Avoid: the runway fence

Stay away from the fence at the end of the runway during takeoffs. A tourist was killed by jet blast there in July 2017, and police said dozens had been injured in earlier years. The posted signs warn of extreme bodily harm or death. Watch from the bar, not the fence.

Mullet Bay

BeachDIY friendlyFar from port

Mullet Bay sits right next to Maho, so it pairs naturally with plane spotting if you want proper beach time too. It shares Maho's traffic zone, which means a drive of about 30 minutes on a clear road and an hour or more on a busy one.

Mullet Bay Beach on Google Maps

Getting there and back

The official taxi fare from the cruise port to either Maho or Mullet Bay is $25 for one or two people. Shared vans with a return wristband run about $15 to $20 per person round trip. On a heavy ship day, this is the corner of the island most worth skipping.

French Side Beaches: Orient Bay and Pinel Island

The island's longest beach, and a small island you reach by ferry.

Orient Bay

BeachDIY friendlyCruise excursionFar from port

About seven miles north of the pier, Orient Bay is a mile and a half of soft white sand. It faces the Atlantic, but an offshore reef keeps the water generally clear and calm. A string of beach clubs lines the bay: Coco Beach Club at the north end is the priciest at $60 to $80 for two, while most others charge around $30 for two chairs and an umbrella, sometimes $40.

Orient Bay Beach on Google Maps

Tip: The far south end, near Club Orient, is a nude section. Sargassum seaweed sometimes washes in, though the clubs rake regularly. Bernard's Tours runs a $35 per person Orient Beach shuttle with a guaranteed return, leaving at 10:00 am and giving you three and a half to four hours on the sand; chairs aren't included.

Pinel Island

BeachNatureCruise excursionPrivate tour optionHigh timing risk

Pinel is a small island off French Cul-de-Sac, and the only way over is by small ferry; taxis can take you only as far as the landing. The water around it is a marine reserve, and snorkel gear rents on the main beach. On cruise days that main beach fills fast.

Pinel ferry dock on Google Maps

Tip: The last ferry back is reported anywhere from 4:00 pm to 4:40 pm depending on source and season. Confirm it at the dock the moment you arrive. You can also rent a kayak near the dock and paddle across.

Pinel the easy way

Discover SXM runs an all-inclusive Pinel tour with port pickup, a private boat, reserved chairs, a guided hike, guided snorkeling and lunch, listed at $189 for adults and $150 for children. It takes about six hours, so it only makes sense if your ship sails at 5:00 pm or later.

Marigot, Fort Louis and Loterie Farm

The French capital is a town to walk, not a beach to lie on.

Fort Louis

DIY friendlyHistoric siteFar from port

The French built Fort Louis in 1789 on the hill above Marigot. The walls are intact and the cannons still in place, and the view takes in Marigot Bay, Simpson Bay Lagoon and Anguilla to the north. The climb is 91 steps, about 15 minutes from the waterfront.

Fort Louis on Google Maps

Tip: Go up in the morning, before the heat builds.

Marigot Market

DIY friendlyCultural experienceFar from port

The open-air waterfront market at the foot of the fort is at its fullest on Wednesday and Saturday mornings, with produce, spices, crafts and clothing. One port guide lists hours of 6:00 am to 1:00 pm and notes that bargains are few. On market days, coachloads of cruise passengers pack the stalls.

Marigot Market on Google Maps

Tip: Keep a close hand on your belongings in the crowds. Marigot prices in euros, and paying in dollars or by card can effectively cost you 15 to 20 percent more.

Loterie Farm

NatureCruise excursionFar from portWatch the clock

A former 18th-century sugar plantation at the foot of Pic Paradis, now an eco-park with tree-canopy zip lines, a spring-fed swimming area and hiking trails. A one-hour hike climbs from the farm to Pic Paradis, and the Fly Zone Extreme course is for ages 18 and up. It's 30 to 40 minutes from the cruise port.

Loterie Farm on Google Maps

Tip: Arrive early to get ahead of the cruise groups.

Don't pack a towel for Marigot

Marigot has no beach worth visiting. Treat it as a morning of fort, market and waterfront, then decide whether you have time for anything else.

Taxis and Private Tours

Fares are fixed, meters don't exist, and the price drops as your group grows.

Taxi fares are set by the government, zone to zone. There's no Uber or other rideshare on the island. Here are the official fares from the cruise port:

Philipsburg$10$4$3
Little Bay$10$5$5

Orient Bay and Marigot aren't on the official table. Quotes for two people run roughly $30 to $45 to Orient Bay and about $20 to Marigot, or around $10 per person in a shared van. Fares rise 25 percent after 10 pm and 50 percent after midnight.

Private island tours

Private tour option

Island-tour kiosks sit just outside the main port gates, and customizable private tours are sold online. Typical routes take in Mullet Bay, Grand Case, Orient Beach and Maho. Groups have the most leverage: one traveler in a party of seven saw a four-hour tour drop from $45 to $25 per person.

Agree on two things before you get in

First, the price, and whether it's per person or per car; some drivers add "per person" on arrival. Second, the clock. Independent operators run on local island time, which may not match your ship's time, so give them your all-aboard time and confirm which clock you're both using.

Good to Know

A few practical notes that save time and money ashore.

Money

The Dutch side's currency is the Caribbean guilder (XCG), which replaced the old Antillean guilder in 2025 and is pegged at US$1 = 1.79 XCG. In practice, US dollars are accepted almost everywhere and most prices are shown in dollars. Bring small bills for beach chairs, vendors and tips. The French side uses euros.

Safety

The US State Department rates both Sint Maarten and French Saint Martin at Level 1, "exercise normal precautions," the lowest advisory level. Ordinary care applies: watch your bag in crowded markets and don't leave anything in a parked car.

One rule worth knowing

It is illegal to bring guns, ammunition, or even spent shells or casings into Sint Maarten. If you keep a souvenir casing in your bag, leave it on the ship.
