Roatán, Honduras

A one-day guide to Coxen Hole, built for your stop on the Norwegian Escape.

Norwegian Escape docks at the Port of Roatan in Coxen Hole — you may also hear this terminal called "Town Center at Port of Roatan" or "Roatan Village." It's the only Roatán port NCL uses; if you've read guides that mention Mahogany Bay, that's a different terminal used by Carnival-family ships, about 7 km away, and none of that applies to your day here.

Arriving in Coxen Hole

The first hour of your day depends on whether you walk off the ship or ride in on a tender.

The terminal here sits right in the middle of Coxen Hole's town center — you'll step off and see shops and shore excursion information immediately. It's a small setup: restrooms, a pharmacy, an ATM, internet access, an information booth, a taxi stand, a bank, a large Diamonds International, a couple of restaurants, and a liquor store. The port itself is flat and easy to walk around, so finding your tour group or taxi shouldn't be a problem.

Dock or tender — confirm it before you plan your day

Port of Roatan can hold three ships at once — one alongside the pier, two at anchor. Royal Caribbean ships typically get dock priority when schedules overlap, which means Norwegian ships are the ones more likely to tender. November through April is peak season, and Mondays, Wednesdays, and Thursdays are the days most Norwegian ships end up tendering here. Check with the ship the morning of — it changes how much buffer you need to build into anything with a fixed pickup time.

Walking out of the terminal gate into Coxen Hole town itself is a different experience than walking around inside it — more on that below, in what to know before you go.

Getting Around

Everything worth doing today is a taxi, a water taxi, or a private driver away.

The island splits into five taxi zones with posted rates: Zone A covers Coxen Hole and French Harbour at $20 per cab for up to four people. Zone B — French Cay, Black Pearl Golf, Sandy Bay — runs $10 per person with a two-person minimum. Zone C, which is West End and West Bay, runs $15 per person, also with a two-person minimum. Zones D and E, further out toward Oak Ridge, Punta Gorda, Diamond Rock, and Camp Bay, run $15–$20 per person.

Independent reports land a bit lower for the West Bay and West End runs specifically, and rates aren't fixed by any government agency, so it's worth confirming the price before you get in — dockside taxis tend to charge more than what you'd pay once you're away from the pier. Figure at least $5 per person to West Bay, with a $50 round-trip minimum from Coxen Hole as a starting point for negotiation. One traveler reported paying $40 total for a private van seating six, round trip to West Bay, booked ahead through WhatsApp. Arranging a taxi is almost always a round-trip deal — the driver waits and brings you back to the pier when you're ready.

The water taxi is the move between West End and West Bay

If your day includes both West End and West Bay, skip the road — the water taxi between them runs about $2–3 per person, takes 10–15 minutes, and is cheaper if you pay in lempira. It's not on a fixed schedule, so you catch it where you're dropped off and it'll come back around.

Two bus routes run from Coxen Hole out to West End Bay for about $2.60 one-way, roughly a 10-minute ride. If you're in a larger group, a "Rapidito" — a minibus that seats up to 12 — keeps everyone together for one fare. And if you'd rather not manage any of this yourself, a private driver booked ahead is worth considering: one traveler put together a five-hour custom day — island tour, ziplining, monkeys and sloths, a souvenir stop, chocolate and rum tastings, and a swim at West Bay Beach — for $65 per person, which holds up well against what the cruise line charges for less.

What to Know Before You Go

This is a safety-sensitive stop, and it's worth being straightforward about it rather than glossing over it.

The U.S. State Department's country summary for Honduras notes that violent crime — homicide, armed robbery, kidnapping — remains common nationally, with gang activity widespread. Around the Bay Islands specifically — Roatán, Utila, Guanaja — there's a concentration of security resources, and these areas are policed more heavily than the mainland. Honduras carries a Level 3 "Reconsider Travel" advisory as a country, which is a broad, country-wide rating that doesn't map cleanly onto a single heavily-touristed cruise stop — but it's the honest starting point rather than something to skip past.

Coxen Hole itself — outside the port gate

This is where accounts diverge the most from the rest of the island. One NCL passenger described the crowd of vendors right at the gate as overwhelming — hands reaching in before she'd even stepped outside, and being surrounded by people offering unsolicited "tours" once she did. She did find one genuine local crafts store worth the walk, and noted beers going for $1 in town — so it's not nothing, but the ratio of hassle to payoff runs high. Coxen Hole gets busy and packed, and there have been reports of petty theft and minor incidents, so the standard advice applies: keep your belongings secure, stay aware, avoid poorly lit areas, and don't walk around alone at night. One long-time visitor called an early walk into town "very chaotic," with a lot of pressure to buy things — though another visitor who goes regularly for groceries said she's never had an issue. One port-rating aggregator scores Coxen Hole 6.5 out of 10 for safety, ranking it 105th of 131 Caribbean cruise ports — "generally considered safe," with a police and security presence, but still worth going in with your eyes open.

Where to put your energy instead

Every source in this research points the same direction: treat Coxen Hole town as optional rather than a planned stop, and put your time into West Bay, West End, and organized excursions or private tours — all of which read as consistently safer and more worth your time than wandering the streets right outside the gate.

A few general precautions worth carrying with you regardless of where you go: don't bring large amounts of cash or jewelry off the ship, use the ship's safe for anything you're not using that day, avoid isolated areas and nighttime walking, and confirm your taxi fare before you get in rather than after. If you're booking a tour, book it through a vetted operator rather than someone who approaches you directly outside the port — and don't cut your return time closer to the ship's departure than you need to.

Where to Spend Your Day

The best of Roatán, on a day when you have to be back by a certain hour.

West Bay Beach

Beach · Cruise excursion · DIY friendly

West Bay Beach, on the island's southwestern tip, is Roatán's most well-known beach — extensive white sand, resorts and restaurants lining the shore, and a barrier reef close enough to snorkel from land. Most cruise excursions specifically target the Tabyana Beach section, about a 15-minute drive from Coxen Hole. Entrance runs about $10 per person and typically gets you a chair or a drink, plus access to bathrooms and changing rooms. The reef sits just 70 feet offshore — Caribbean Travel and Life ranked this the 4th-best beach in the world for snorkeling.

Bring your own towel — it's rarely included with beach entrance fees here.

Gumbalimba Park

Cruise excursion · Private tour option · Nature

Gumbalimba Park, in West Bay, is roughly 25 minutes from the port. Admission covers a guide, Coxen's pirate cave, the animal preserve, the botanical garden, the beach area, lounge chairs, showers, a freshwater pool, and parking — zipline, snorkel gear, and kayaking cost extra. You'll walk through with capuchin monkeys and free-flying macaws and parrots close enough to interact with, an air-conditioned insectarium holding around 2,500 preserved insects, and a suspension bridge. General admission runs about $30, up to $55 if you add the zipline. Reviews are consistently positive on the animal encounters and the zipline itself, with one recurring caveat worth passing along honestly: it reads as "a bit commercial" rather than a pure nature reserve, and you should go in with that expectation rather than a wilder one.

The canopy zipline

Cruise excursion · Nature

Gumbalimba's zipline is one of only two certified canopy tours in Honduras built on a three-cable system, and it runs $45 on its own, with the option to add full Gumbalimba Park access for $10 more. The course crosses an open lagoon near the end. Wear closed-toe shoes and pants or shorts — not flip-flops.

The dolphin encounter is a real experience, but know the format going in

The Roatán Institute for Marine Sciences, part of Anthony's Key Resort in Sandy Bay, has been studying coral reefs and coastal marine life since 1989 and runs dolphin interaction programs across two enclosures — one at Bailey's Key, one on the main island. The standard Dolphin Encounter is a 30-minute, waist-deep session with a trainer and a dolphin, including jumps, tail walks, and photos. Children 8 and under need a paying adult with them, and pregnant women can't participate. You'll transfer by bus and then take a short boat ride across the lagoon to Bailey's Key. Pricing runs roughly $103–$129 per person depending on whether transportation is bundled in, and travelers who did the free-swim/snorkel version rated it especially highly. Worth naming plainly: this is a captive-dolphin facility, not a wild encounter — if that distinction matters to you, factor it in before you book.

Little French Key

Beach · Cruise excursion · Private tour option

Little French Key is a small private island reached by a roughly 20-minute van ride from the terminals, followed by a short boat crossing — you'll check in at the Frenchy's 44 dock, get a wristband, and board the boat over. A basic transport-plus-entry pass runs about $55, with food and drinks purchased separately; a $10 add-on gets you onto the dive boat needed to snorkel past the reef, since swimming out to it from shore isn't really practical. Most reviews are enthusiastic about the beach and the animal sanctuary, though one traveler flagged sand flea bites as a real hazard — sores that were still visible a week later. Book directly through the resort's official website rather than a reseller; there's been past confusion with unofficial copycat sites using a similar name.

West End Village

DIY friendly · Walkable once you're there

West End is the most walkable village on the island — a sheltered cove, the highest concentration of dive shops on Roatán, restaurants lined up along the waterfront, and a low-key but real nightlife once the sun goes down. The Roatan Rum Company distillery sits at the top of a hill here, with the El Faro lighthouse directly across the street — worth the small entry fee for the view. If you're arriving by taxi or water taxi rather than a packaged excursion, this is a relaxed, walkable alternative to spending your time in Coxen Hole town.

Roatán Chocolate Factory & Roatán Rum Company

Cruise excursion · DIY friendly · Cultural experience

The chocolate factory is a small-scale Honduran operation using organic Criollo and Trinitario cacao sourced from the Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage site — the operation frames itself around supporting local indigenous communities and rainforest protection. At the Rum Company, you get free samples of artisan rums and rum cakes, plus access to a "Blendatorium" where you mix your own flavored rum from more than 50 extracts and take home a personalized bottle. Rums run $25–$35, rum cakes $7–$39. These two are frequently paired as a single 45–90 minute stop, low-risk and easy to slot in before or after a beach afternoon.

Half Moon Bay

Beach · DIY friendly

Half Moon Bay sits about 20 minutes from the cruise port, right off West End's main strip, and is known locally for calm, shallow water and a good spot to watch the sun go down — though on a port day you'll be seeing it well before sunset. Easy to fold into a West End stop rather than treating it as a separate destination.

What to Skip This Trip

Not every good thing on Roatán fits inside a single port day.

French Harbour and the nearby iguana farm at Fantasy Island are genuinely interesting — over 2,800 iguanas across four species living on one islander's property, alongside the island's largest fishing fleet — but it's a further drive out, and not essential if your time is tight today. The same goes for deep-water excursions or advanced dive certification courses: worthwhile if you have extra hours in port, but the scheduling risk is real if conditions or logistics run long, and it's not worth gambling your return time on.

One note to avoid confusion

If you come across a Roatán guide online that mentions Mahogany Bay attractions, that's a different terminal entirely, used by Carnival-family ships — it has nothing to do with your day on the Norwegian Escape, which docks at Coxen Hole.

Timing Your Day

The logistics that decide whether your afternoon goes smoothly.

If the ship ends up tendering rather than docking, build extra buffer into anything with a fixed pickup time — tender lines can eat 30 to 60 minutes or more, unpredictably, on both ends of your day. Independent tour operators consistently state that you need to be back in port no later than one hour before the ship's departure time — that's their own buffer, not the cruise line's actual all-aboard time, so check the ship's posted time specifically rather than relying on the tour operator's number alone.

Little French Key, the dolphin encounter at RIMS, and Gumbalimba Park all involve a boat transfer or multiple legs, which carries more built-in timing risk than a straight taxi ride to a beach. Private and cruise-line-booked excursions generally come with a "back to ship" guarantee that independent DIY bookings don't. If you're building your own day with taxis and water taxis, West Bay and West End are the lowest-risk, most flexible option for a beach day without a packaged tour.

Money and manners

The lempira is the official currency, but U.S. dollars are widely accepted in tourist areas — just expect change back in lempira. Carry small bills. A 10–20% tip is standard at restaurants and for taxis, and haggling is expected at duty-free shops and souvenir stalls.
