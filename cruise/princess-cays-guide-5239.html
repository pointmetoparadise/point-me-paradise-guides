Princess Cays, Bahamas

Half a mile of soft sand at the southern tip of Eleuthera, and a full day to do as much or as little as you like.

Coral Princess  ·  Friday, October 30, 2026  ·  In port 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM

Princess Cays isn't a separate island. It's a 40-acre stretch of beachfront carved out of Eleuthera's southern tip, fenced off for cruise guests, with a marina in the middle and walkways linking everything. There are no waterslides and no big pool. The day is about the water, which is often reported as clear enough to see your feet in chest-deep water.

This guide walks you through getting ashore, choosing your side of the beach, what's included, what costs extra, and the few ways to see more of Eleuthera before the last tender.

Getting Ashore

Your ship anchors offshore, and a tender boat carries you in.

The water off Princess Cays is too shallow for a cruise ship to dock, so you'll ride a tender to shore. The trip typically takes 5 to 15 minutes, depending on where the ship anchors and how quickly boats load. You'll step off at a small marina in the middle of the resort.

Open Princess Cays Marina in Google Maps

Tip: Sit at either end of the tender for a clear photo of the ship on the way in.

Left or right?

The marina splits the resort in two. About two-thirds of it lies to your left: the shops, the larger food pavilion, several small coves, the bungalows, and most of the people. The right side is smaller and usually quieter. It has Serenity Beach, loungers, a sport court, and its own food pavilion.

Either way, the crowd tends to cluster near the seating closest to the marina. Walk a few minutes in either direction and it thins out.

Weather can change the plan

Tendering depends on wind and swell. In rough conditions the ship may shorten or cancel the call, and that decision is the captain's to make. Watch for announcements the morning you arrive.

A quick check before you sail

Look at your itinerary in Manage Booking on princess.com about two weeks before departure, and again before you leave home. If anything about this call changes, Princess notifies booked guests directly.

The Beach

The sand and the water are the reason to come.

Serenity Beach and the coves

The beach runs in a narrow strip about half a mile long, broken into six small coves where you can sit right at the water's edge. The sand is soft and white, and the water is shallow and clear. Reports on the sand vary a little: some describe it as powdery, others as slightly coarser than other Bahamian beaches. Near the bungalows at the far left, the shoreline turns rocky.

Open Serenity Beach in Google Maps

Tip: Pack water shoes. You'll want them for the rocky stretches toward the bungalows.

Snorkeling from shore

You can snorkel without a boat here. The right side of the property is typically reported to have the better underwater views, with coral and fish close in. Longtime visitors suggest entering the water on the right, swimming out to the buoy line, and following it to the right. You can rent gear on the island or bring your own.

The observation tower

A wooden lookout tower gives you a view over the beach and the water from above. It's a short climb and a good way to get your bearings before you choose a spot.

Finding shade

Free loungers are everywhere, but most don't have umbrellas. For shade you can rent a clamshell, daybed, or bungalow, or claim a lounger under a tree. Natural shade goes early, so an early tender helps.

Food, Drinks & Shopping

Lunch is included; drinks and souvenirs are not.

The beach barbecue

Two food pavilions, one on each side of the marina, serve the same complimentary lunch: salads, burgers, hot dogs, and a local-style chicken and rice. Lines get long at midday, and there isn't always shade while you wait. There are several serving lines, and the crowd doesn't always notice, so if yours looks unusually long, check the others.

Tip: Food typically opens around 11:00 AM. Be there when it opens, or wait until after 1:00 PM.

Bars and drinks

Bars sit along the length of the beach, and servers walk the sand taking orders, so you rarely have to get up. Purchases go on your cruise card. Guests sailing with Princess have often reported that their beverage package carried over ashore, but confirm with guest services before your first order.

Shops and local vendors

The resort-run shops charge to your cruise card. Local vendors work from carts and kiosks near the restrooms, selling crafts and souvenirs and offering hair braiding, and they typically take cash only.

Bring small bills

A few small U.S. bills cover the local vendors, the braiders, and any stand outside the gate. Everything run by the resort goes on your cruise card.

Rentals & Bungalows

Most of the extras here are gear and shade, not tours.

Private bungalows

Air-conditioned bungalows line the left side of the resort, and a tram runs bungalow guests between their bungalow and the marina. The main-beach bungalows work well for families. The Sanctuary bungalows sit at the very far left in a private, adults-only (18+) area. Prices have been reported from roughly $210 to $250 for up to four guests, but rates change, so check current pricing when you book.

Tip: Bungalows are limited and usually sell out before sailing, so book early. The water access in front of some of them is rocky.

Clamshells and water toys

A clamshell, a two-person sun shelter, typically runs around $30. You can also rent snorkel gear and vests, one- or two-person ocean kayaks, stand-up paddleboards, four-person paddle wheelers, lockers, and water noodles, or book a banana boat ride.

Read the fine print

Princess sells Princess Cays tours, rentals, and water sports as non-refundable once the cancellation deadline passes. Book what you're sure about, and check the deadline in your booking.

On the Water

Every water excursion here leaves from the marina, a few minutes' walk from your lounger.

Stingray Beach Encounter

About 1 hourAges 15+

This is the most popular excursion at Princess Cays. You wade into shallow water and pet and feed the rays, with a guide alongside. Departures run throughout the day, and recent guests have singled out the guides for praise.

Glass-Bottom Boat Cruise

About 1 hourStays dry

A one-hour ride from the marina out to a coral reef, watching the fish through the hull. It's the easy choice if you don't swim, don't want to get wet, or have small children with you.

Princess Cays Coastal Cruise

About 1 hour

A short boat ride along Eleuthera's coves and rocky shoreline, seen from the water. It's relaxed, with nothing to do but look.

Snorkeling at Princess Cays (Experienced Snorkelers)

1.5 hoursAges 12+Listed from $59.95

A 42-foot boat cruises along Eleuthera's southern coast to a shallow reef, where you get about an hour in the water. Gear and a snorkel vest are included, the vest must be worn, and the boat has freshwater showers.

Tip: No instruction is given. If you're new to snorkeling, start from the beach instead.

Lagoon Clear-Bottom Kayaking

You paddle a clear-bottom kayak across calm lagoon water and watch the marine life pass underneath. It's gentler than ocean kayaking and a good fit for a first-time paddler.

Surf Fishing

About 2.5 hours

You cast from the rocky shoreline at the edge of the resort and try for local fish. It's a quiet, unhurried way to spend part of the morning.

Book water activities through the ship

The U.S. State Department notes that boating in the Bahamas is not well regulated. On a tender day, booking through Princess also means the timing is coordinated with the ship, so stick with the ship's own water activities here.

Beyond the Gate

There is more of Eleuthera past the fence, but the ship's tours are the way to see it.

Dune Buggy Adventure & Cultural Heritage Tour

1.5 hoursAges 10–75Max 265 lbsListed from $169.95

You walk from the marina to the resort gates, climb into a buggy, and follow your guide down the bumpy back roads of south Eleuthera. The route includes about 20 minutes at a pink-sand beach. It also passes Bannerman Town, a small settlement named for Sir Alexander Bannerman, governor of the Bahamas in the 1850s. You stop for photos at the ruins of the Anglican Church of the Assumption, built by enslaved people in the 18th century. The ruins are a reminder of the island's history of slavery, and you'll want to visit them with that in mind.

Tip: Drivers must be 21 or older with a valid driver's license, so bring it ashore. Expect dust, and wear clothes you don't mind getting dirty.

Rock Sound & Eleuthera Scenic Drive

An air-conditioned drive north takes you through open country and small villages to Rock Sound, south Eleuthera's largest settlement. Stops include St. Luke's Anglican Church, a seaside church dating to the 1800s, and the Ocean Hole, an inland pool of seawater that rises and falls with the tides even though the sea is about a mile away. When school is in session, the tour may also visit a local primary school. Past versions of this tour have run about four hours, so check the current listing for the duration before you book.

Just outside the gate

A short walk past the resort gates, there has typically been a small straw market with handmade goods from local artisans. Some vendors there sell Bahamian beers like Kalik and Sands, and visitors have reported drinks at a little stand outside the gate for less than resort prices. Whether you can step out depends on the day, and you may be asked to show your cruise card to leave and come back in. Ask a crew member at the gate.

Don't head off on your own

There's no town near Princess Cays and no taxi stand at the gate, and the nearest settlements are a long drive away. Private arrangements carry no guarantee the ship will wait, and on a tender day that margin disappears fast. If you want to see Eleuthera, book the ship's tour.

Traveling with Kids

This is a beach day, not a water park.

Younger children get a small playground with a kid-sized wading pool. Older kids can shoot hoops or play volleyball on the sport courts, and the shallow, calm water near shore suits beginners. For a no-swim outing, the glass-bottom boat is the easiest choice. The stingray encounter is limited to ages 15 and up.

Pack extra towels

Princess provides beach towels from the ship, but with children an extra one or two never goes to waste.

Planning Your Day

Seven hours goes quickly when part of it is spent in tender lines.

A simple shape for the day

  • Morning: Take an early tender, choose your side, and claim shade while it's still available. Book a morning excursion if you want one; they tend to be less crowded.
  • Around 11:00 AM: Eat as soon as the barbecue opens, or hold off until after 1:00 PM.
  • Afternoon: Snorkel, nap, and use the rentals you booked.
  • Leaving: Check the last tender time posted onboard. It comes well before the 4:00 PM departure, and the walk back from the far left plus the line can take a while. Start packing up at least 45 minutes before you want to be on a tender.

Getting around and accessibility

Everything inside the resort is reached on foot along paved walkways, with a tram for bungalow guests. Tender boarding, deep sand, and a footbridge can make independent wheelchair use difficult. Crew can assist, but Princess staff aren't permitted to lift more than 50 pounds. If mobility is a concern, ask Princess before you sail about tender eligibility, beach wheelchairs, and accessible excursions.

Staying connected

One recent visitor found that the ship's Wi-Fi stopped working ashore, but a free, open network was available on the beach. Treat any open network as public, and don't log into banking or anything sensitive.

Safety

The U.S. State Department lists the Bahamas at Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution." Most of the crime it cites occurs in Nassau and Freeport, far from this gated resort. Use the same sense you would at any beach. Follow the beach flags and the staff's instructions, watch for currents and coral, and drink more water than you think you need.

What to bring ashore

Reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, water shoes, your cruise card, small U.S. bills, your driver's license if you're driving a dune buggy, and snorkel gear if you have your own.
