1 · Before you leave

Passport, Portugal & Spain

[DOCUMENT NEEDED] Assuming U.S. passports, Portugal and Spain permit tourism without a visa for up to 90 days in a rolling 180-day Schengen period. Each passport should have at least three months’ validity after your November 5 departure; six months is a prudent cushion. Portugal specifies two blank pages. Check each name against every reservation.

ETIAS is not accepting applications as of this guide’s September 13 research; verify its status shortly before October 23 at the official EU website. Do not pay an unofficial site. The separate EU Entry/Exit System may involve a photo and fingerprints on arrival. Portugal and Spain are both Schengen, so a Salamanca outing does not usually involve a routine passport booth.

No vaccination certificate is listed as a routine entry requirement for Portugal. Consult your clinician for individualized advice and carry prescription medicines in original containers.

8 · The practical things that span the whole trip

Money & tipping

[CURRENCY NOTE] Portugal and Spain use euros; cards work widely but carry small notes for cafés, rural stops and taxis. Choose EUR when a terminal offers dollars (dynamic currency conversion is usually worse). Portuguese restaurant service is discretionary rather than a U.S.-style mandatory percentage; check the bill before adding a modest tip. Tip independent guides/drivers for good service as you wish. Check your A-ROSA fare documents for onboard crew gratuities—do not assume shore and onboard rules are identical.

Power, phones & time

Portugal and Spain use type C/F plugs and 230V; use an adapter and check your device’s input label before plugging in hair tools. Ask staff what outlets exist in D-311. One Portugal/Spain-capable EU eSIM or international roaming package is simpler than two local SIMs; download offline maps and tickets. The ship follows its posted clock, which should be checked before every excursion. Portugal changes from summer to winter time October 25; Spain is generally one hour ahead of mainland Portugal. Detroit/Newark change to U.S. standard time November 1; rely on local clocks and airline/ship displays, not a fixed time difference.

Pack for the transitions

Bring layered outfits for cool river mornings/evenings, mild-to-wet city days and windy sunset boating; two pairs of supportive nonslip shoes for cobbles and gangways; light raincoat, compact day bag, sunglasses, refillable bottle. No formal attire is listed. Keep a small cash reserve, passport, insurer contact, prescriptions and one change of clothes in carry-on. One medium case each makes four handovers—airport, hotel, trains, ship—much easier. Power banks and spare lithium batteries belong in cabin baggage, not checked luggage.

Safety & help

For police/ambulance/fire in Portugal and Spain dial 112; in the U.S. dial 911. Save the Faye policy and A-ROSA reception/contact details offline. Crowds around Lisbon viewpoints and train stations reward zipped bags and awareness. Ask A-ROSA about their onboard medical support and evacuation procedures; inland river care may mean a shore provider. Call your insurer’s assistance line for any significant medical issue. Avoid overcommitting to steep old-town walking after full-day excursions.
